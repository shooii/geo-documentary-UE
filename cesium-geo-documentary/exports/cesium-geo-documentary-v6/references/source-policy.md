# Source policy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Government, official statistics, and geographic authorities.
2. Museums, archives, and cultural heritage institutions.
3. Academic publications.
4. Authoritative encyclopedias.
5. Reputable feature reporting.

Store claim text, title, URL, publisher, publication/access dates, confidence, usage, and quote-limit status. Add the year whenever population, GDP, administrative status, or listings may change.

For geographic hierarchy, store the authoritative parent-child list, effective date, normalized level role, official native level name, and inclusion policy. For narrated physical features, require a source that supports both the name and geometry. For history/culture images, require a source that supports the exact locality and subject as well as a reusable license.

Do not turn inference into fact. Lower certainty in narration when sources conflict or are incomplete. Treat administrative boundaries as visualization data unless a lawful authoritative boundary source is supplied.

Do not fabricate citations or reuse a homepage URL as evidence for a claim not present there.

Search results are discovery aids, not evidence or reuse permission. Reject an image whose subject is right but locality is unverified, or whose locality is right but the narrated cultural form is wrong.

Visible source cards are optional presentation elements, not a substitute for the research record. In clean-broadcast mode, keep citations and asset licenses in external reports instead of placing `来源` text on screen. Retain any attribution that a map or photo license legally requires.
